The church counting down to the death of intellectual property

The Church of Conceptual Art has set a public date for the end of intellectual property law.

Press kit · October 2026 · Download as PDF · paul@paulpissarro.com

The story

The Church of Conceptual Art (CoCA) is a religious nonprofit incorporated in Montana, founded by the artists Paul-Alexander Pissarro and Jesse Do. Its stated purpose is "to lead people to clarity through the primacy of the idea or concept." It maintains a public countdown to what it calls the Death of IP, the end of intellectual property law.

The church's founding text, The Art Bible of the Church of Conceptual Art, Volume I: The Great Deprecation (2026), sets out the doctrine behind the date. Faith-Based Jurisdiction holds that a religiously incorporated entity can declare appropriated corporate symbols sacred. Liturgical Appropriation names the act by which a corporate logo is converted from economic capital into symbolic capital. Whether this is a legal theory, a theology, or a long-form artwork is a question the church declines to resolve.

The Bible is a real book with a Library of Congress record (LCCN 2026001210), catalogued under the subject heading New Corporate Realism (Art movement). The countdown is running now.
