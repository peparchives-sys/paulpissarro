Heaven costs $777. Hell costs $666.

The Church of Conceptual Art has opened ticketing for the afterlife.

Press kit · October 2026 · Download as PDF · paul@paulpissarro.com

The story

In July 2026 the Church of Conceptual Art opened ticketing for the afterlife at finaldues.com. The standing schedule lists a Ticket to Heaven at $777, a Ticket to Hell at $666, and Both Gates at $1,443; during the closing period, the digital-only, hash-verified tickets clear at $7.77, $6.66 and $14.43. A Fast Pass Upgrade at the full standing price ($777 Heaven, $666 Hell) is a physical, made-to-order credential: key set, certificate and frame. Passes are named, non-transferable and never expire, and can be bought for oneself, as a gift, or as unnamed bearer passes that admit whoever holds them.

Payment is the only qualification; there is no review of conduct. Every sale is recorded in a public, append-only ledger built on a SHA-256 hash chain that anyone can recompute. The record page shows the ledger live, with running counts for each gate, and every certificate carries the holder's name, destination and the hash of its entry. The passes are declared conceptual art objects conferring no rights or services in this life, and the terms state it plainly: all sales are final, both in this life and the next.

From the announcement: "Every religion in history has sold this product," said Paul-Alexander Pissarro, co-founder of the Church of Conceptual Art. "We are simply the first to publish the price, the terms, and the ledger." The church, he added, "asks you to be a customer, which the record suggests is the more honest relationship."

The site runs deeper than a checkout. The Argument makes the church's case; The Precedent keeps a register of the prior arrangements religions have sold; The Ballot holds a daily General Election of the Soul. The program is the church's first sacrament available at retail, issued through Unsubscribe NYC LLC in collaboration with Super//Natural. Gate intake, processing speed and audits remain at the discretion of the afterlife administrations, which did not respond to press inquiries.
