The argument

The European degree ladder is a craft-guild structure transposed onto knowledge. Bachelor, master and doctor correspond to apprentice, journeyman and master, and doctoral training was formally an apprenticeship, with a customary seven-year term matching the apprenticeship of other medieval trades.

Master and doctor were originally synonymous. Both named one thing: the licentia docendi, the licence to teach. Neither certified an original contribution to knowledge. Both certified admission to the body licensed to instruct, and that licence was an ecclesiastical instrument, centralising authority over who might teach and therefore over what could be taught.

The definition of the doctorate as certifying new knowledge is a modern retrofit. When the institution says a doctorate proves a contribution, it is dressing a guild-entry rite in Enlightenment language. This thesis argues that the credential was never bound to the contribution, that the two can be separated without loss, and that separating them is itself demonstrable rather than merely arguable.
