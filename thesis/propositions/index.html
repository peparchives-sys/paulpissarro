Pre-deposit. Labels are permanent and already citable by label. The propositions themselves are not final until the work is deposited.

Every load-bearing claim in the work, stated once and labelled permanently. Labels are assigned once and never reused: new propositions append, they do not insert, because insertion would renumber everything after it and break citations already made. A proposition later found to be wrong keeps its number and is marked withdrawn, with the reason given.

A proposition marked cite source is somebody else's finding, restated here only so it can be found. Cite the source named beneath it. A proposition marked cite this work is an inference made here, and the sources beneath it are what it rests on rather than where it came from. The form is the work's identifier and the label, for example Pissarro 2026, C2. Cite the label rather than the page: labels are permanent, pages are not.

Band A · The historical record

Findings established by others, restated for navigation.

A1

The European degree ladder is a craft-guild structure transposed onto knowledge: bachelor, master and doctor correspond to the apprentice, journeyman and master of the medieval trades.

rashdall-1936, verger-1973 cite source

A2

Doctoral training was formally an apprenticeship to a guild, and the customary term of study before admission to the guild of Master of Arts was seven years, matching the apprenticeship term of other medieval occupations.

rashdall-1936 cite source

A3

Master and doctor were originally synonymous. Both named a single thing, the licentia docendi, the licence to teach.

rashdall-1936, verger-1973 cite source

A4

Neither degree originally certified an original contribution to knowledge. Both certified admission to the body licensed to teach.

rashdall-1936, verger-1973 cite source

A5

The licentia docendi was an ecclesiastical instrument. Papal intervention in 1179 requiring that the licence be granted to the qualified centralised authority over who might teach and, by extension, over what could be taught.

rashdall-1936 cite source

A6

The medieval university secured a near-monopoly over a particular kind of teaching and culture. Closure was a social function of the institution, not a later corruption of it.

verger-1973 cite source

A7

The definition of the doctorate as certifying the production of new knowledge is a modern retrofit onto a medieval licence to teach. It is not inherited from the institution's founding structure.

derived from A1 to A6; rashdall-1936, verger-1973, humboldt-1810 cite this work

A8

Humboldt's memorandum on the organisation of the higher scientific institutions in Berlin, written in 1810 and the source usually credited with the research-university ideal, was left unfinished and was not published in his lifetime.

humboldt-1810 cite source

A9

The memorandum was first published in 1903, in the volume of Humboldt's political writings edited by Bruno Gebhardt for the Prussian Academy of Sciences, ninety-three years after it was written. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording gave 1900 as a date of recovery, which no source supports. The claim now anchors on publication, which is documented.

humboldt-1810, verified against catalogue records cite source

A10

The founding document of the research-university ideal became publicly available approximately a century after the institution it is credited with founding, and therefore cannot have exerted the influence attributed to it during that period. Reclassified October 2026: this inference is already established in the historiography and is no longer claimed here.

ash-2006, paletschek-2001, vom-bruch-1997 cite source

A11

The retrospective attribution of the research-university ideal to Humboldt is itself a recognised object of scholarship, treated in the literature as the Humboldt myth.

ash-1999, ash-2006 cite source

A12

The attribution of a legitimating origin to an institution is a general and recognised phenomenon. Traditions presented as ancient are frequently of recent origin and deliberately composed.

hobsbawm-ranger-1983 cite source

A13

The comparison of identical suggestions given with and without an induction was first run systematically by Hull in 1933. The largest study in that line had thirteen subjects, the dependent measure was response latency on ideomotor suggestions, and subjects in the hypnosis condition had passed an additional screen that those in the waking condition had not. Hull reported that the induction facilitated responses and stated no conclusion about the magnitude of its contribution. A later recomputation of the Hull-era data reports a large effect for one 1930 study, which is the standing objection to B1. Added October 2026 to hold the historical claim that B1 previously carried.

hull-1933, juliani-mohl-2016 cite source

Band B · The experimental record

Findings established by others, at the precision their sources support.

B1

When identical suggestions are administered with and without a hypnotic induction, the induction's contribution to measured response is small and reliably non-zero: pooled across four studies, 1.46 suggestions out of ten, standard error 0.20. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording cited Hull alone and credited him with a conclusion about magnitude that he did not state. The characterisation is Kirsch's, reading a literature that begins in 1961; the number is Martin and Dienes's. Hull ran the first version of the design, which is a historical claim and is now held at A13, together with the objection to this one.

martin-dienes-2019, kirsch-mazzoni-montgomery-2007, glass-barber-1961, barber-calverley-1964 cite source

B2

Hypnotic and non-hypnotic suggestibility are very highly correlated, almost as highly as the scales correlate with themselves on retest, indicating that they are not distinct capacities. Reported coefficients run from 0.53 to 0.82. Amended October 2026: the phrase and the finding are the 2011 consensus statement's, not Hull's; no coefficient of Hull's own could be established.

kirsch-et-al-2011, braffman-kirsch-1999, milling-et-al-2010 cite source

B3

Suggestions given without any induction procedure produce substantial changes in perception, memory and voluntary action.

barber-1969 cite source

B4

Hypnotic responding is accounted for by expectancy, attitude and imaginative involvement, without requiring the postulation of an altered state of consciousness.

kirsch-lynn-1995 cite source

B5

Placebo analgesia and symptom relief survive explicit disclosure that the agent is inert, provided the disclosure is accompanied by a plausible account of why an inert agent may work. The one trial isolating disclosure from rationale found that disclosure alone produced no effect. Pooled open-label placebo effects are moderate on self-report, 0.36 standardised mean difference, and null on objective outcomes, 0.02. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording asserted that disclosure does not abolish the mechanism, which the evidence does not support at that strength, and cited no publication. No controlled test of disclosure against concealment exists in hypnosis research; the inference from placebo to suggestion is an argument from analogy and is marked as one.

locher-et-al-2017, fendel-et-al-2025, kirsch-1996 cite source

B6

Given the same suggestion with and without an induction, subjects report comparable perceptual effects, but report having been hypnotised only where an induction was performed, and the sense that the response was involuntary rises measurably with it. In three independent datasets the induction is inert for the outcome and not inert for the phenomenology. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording said the ceremony alters the label rather than the result, which understates its own evidence. The shift in reported involuntariness is measurable and significant.

kirsch-et-al-2008, milling-et-al-2010, kekecs-et-al-2024 cite source

B7

A class of utterances does not describe a state of affairs but brings one into being. Naming, promising and founding are acts performed in speech rather than reports about the world.

austin-1962 cite source

B8

Abstract concepts are structured by sensorimotor metaphor drawn from bodily experience. Reason is not separable from the body that carries it.

embodied-cognition cite source

B9

Perception is the resolution of prediction error against prior expectation rather than the passive reception of sensory input.

predictive-processing cite source

B10

Dopaminergic signalling encodes the difference between received and predicted reward. Salience is therefore a function of the gap between expectation and event rather than of magnitude alone.

reward-prediction-error cite source

B11

Emotion is constructed, predicted from interoceptive signals and learned conceptual categories, rather than triggered as a fixed reaction.

constructed-emotion cite source

B12

Aesthetic experiences rated most moving are accompanied by activation in medial prefrontal regions of the default mode network, the system associated with self-referential processing.

dmn-aesthetics cite source

B14

The felt trance state is treated in the consensus literature as an epiphenomenon: a response to the suggestion to enter hypnosis, with no direct causal effect on other suggestions. The experience of nonvolition is not conferred by a trance but is a characteristic of response to suggestion. Markers for the hypothesised state have not been found. B13 was withdrawn before publication and its label is not reused.

kirsch-et-al-2011, lynn-et-al-2020, zahedi-lynn-sommer-2024 cite source

B15

The induction is described in the reference literature as a feature of the hypnotic ritual, and its form does not matter: indirect wording is no more effective than standard, and a doubled induction is no more effective than a single one.

terhune-2024, martin-dienes-2019 cite source

B16

Where the same induction is labelled hypnosis rather than relaxation, subjects are significantly more suggestible. Where subjects are told that hypnotic ability requires achieving a trance, they respond to fewer suggestions. The designation is not inert, and what the designation is taken to mean changes the outcome. Recorded October 2026; the distinction that draws on it is not yet written.

gandhi-oakley-2005, lynn-et-al-2002 cite source

B17

In a four-condition experiment, removing the induction had no appreciable effect on any dimension of responding, while removing the references to cooperation lowered objective responding. The cooperative framing does measurable work where the ritual does not.

lynn-et-al-2002 cite source

B18

Hypnotic responding requires the subject's active participation, and without it nothing happens. The responding is characterised in the literature as a strategic enactment shaped by the subject's understanding of the task, negotiated with the operator. This is the position of a state theorist, who also holds that for highly responsive subjects the induction procedure may be unnecessary.

kihlstrom-2008 cite source

B19

Against B15 and B16: it is argued that the concept of an altered state should be central to any definition of hypnosis, that a relaxation procedure without the label of hypnosis is simply a relaxation procedure, and that hypnosis is therefore not reducible to suggestion. On this account the designation is constitutive rather than descriptive, and the subtraction test does not apply to it, because what remains when the ceremony is removed is a different procedure rather than the same one diminished. It is further argued that a share of hypnotic responding is social compliance rather than co-production. Recorded here because it is the strongest objection to the inferences in Band C and is held by a non-state theorist.

wagstaff-altered-state, wagstaff-1981, wagstaff-1977 cite source

B20

Against B15: highly responsive subjects alter perception with or without an induction, with a small additional benefit from hypnosis, while subjects low in suggestibility succeed in neither condition. Suggestion without ceremony does not produce the phenomena in most people, and trait differences are not abolished by removing the ritual.

mcgeown-et-al-2012, braffman-kirsch-1999 cite source

B21

Against the account of involuntariness in B14: an alternative theory explains the experience of nonvolition through inaccurate higher-order thoughts about intending, without expectancy carrying the explanatory load. No current theory of hypnosis commands assent, and the most recent systematic review calls for new accounts rather than ratifying an existing one.

dienes-perner-2007, zahedi-lynn-sommer-2024 cite source

B22

Against the subtraction inferences generally: the absence of specific neural correlates is held not in itself to argue against classifying hypnosis as an altered state of consciousness.

kihlstrom-2018 cite source

Band C · Distinctions

Inferences drawn in this work from the two records above. Each states what it serves. Seven of them support the claim in Band D; the others support the historical argument, the method, or Pillar IV. A distinction that served nothing would be a defect, not a spare part.

C1

A ceremony that can be removed without changing the capacity it is said to produce is not the cause of that capacity, whatever else its removal changes. Where a ritual is claimed to produce an effect, the test is subtraction, and the test requires naming which effect. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording said “without materially changing an outcome,” which B6 and B14 falsify. Removing the induction leaves the responding intact and removes the reported trance and part of the reported involuntariness. The ceremony does change an outcome. It does not change the capacity.

generalised from B1, B2, B3, B4, B6, B14, B15, B17, B18; contested by B19, B20, B21, B22 cite this work

serves D1

C2

Certification is issued in advance of the use it purports to warrant. It is therefore a forecast of use rather than an instance of it, and stands in the same relation to use as a promise stands to performance.

austin-1962, structural cite this work

serves D1

C3

A promise is a performative that can fail. Certification, being a promise about future use, inherits that liability: it can be issued correctly and still not come true.

austin-1962, derived from B7 and C2 cite this work

serves D1

C4

Attention and citation are distinct quantities. Attention produces an audience and leaves no durable record; citation requires a persistently addressable artifact and produces a countable one. Reach is therefore not evidence of contribution under the discipline's own accounting.

structural cite this work

serves D1

C5

Entry to the principal scholarly indexes is conditioned on the legal form of the depositor, an accredited institution, a selected publisher, or a dues-paying member organisation, rather than on the assessed quality of the work. Access is a property of legal form. The exclusion of unaffiliated scholars is not claimed here and is credited to the authors who established it.

verified against platform documentation October 2026; exclusion per elsabry-2017, tennant-2020, khanna-2022 cite this work

serves D1

C6

A gate set on the legal form of the depositor rather than on the merit of the work is satisfied by incorporation rather than by better work. It therefore screens for the capacity to constitute an organisation, not for the quality it is held to protect, and the cost of satisfying it is a measure of what it actually selects for.

derived from C5, structural cite this work

serves D1

C7

The doctorate's certifying function was added to an instrument designed to license teaching. Certification of contribution is a historical accretion rather than the credential's founding purpose, and cannot therefore be defended by appeal to that purpose.

derived from A1 to A7 cite this work

serves D1

C8

A legitimating origin can be composed after the institution it explains, and the institution's operation is unchanged by its absence. An origin story is therefore subject to the same subtraction test as any other ceremony.

derived from A8 to A12 and C1 cite this work

serves the historical argument, Band A

C9

Even where concealment adds to an effect, what it adds is purchased with the subject's ability to refuse, and that is not a permissible purchase. Disclosure is therefore required whether or not it is costless, and whatever cost it carries is the price of consent rather than a defect in the method. Amended October 2026: the earlier wording asserted that concealment adds nothing to the effect, which the evidence does not support. The argument is now ethical rather than empirical and does not depend on B5 going either way.

structural; B5 and B16 bear on the cost, not on the requirement cite this work

serves the method, and the register the work is written in

C10

A performative utterance's effect is realised as a change in bodily state, not only as a change in belief. If concepts are structured by sensorimotor experience, perception is the resolution of prediction error, and feeling is predicted from interoceptive signal, then an utterance that alters expectation alters the body that holds it. Stated as this work's synthesis: no source named below makes this inference.

derived from B7, B8, B9, B11 cite this work

serves Pillar IV

C11

Attention is allocated by prediction error, and material processed as self-relevant recruits the self-referential network. A work that breaks an expected pattern is therefore both more noticeable and more likely to be processed as being about its reader.
